Section 1: 專案概述Heading (主標): The Restorative Spine
Subheading (副標): A Territorial Cycle Between Manhattan and Hart Island
[Image: 01_161_maiden_lane_physical_model]
Body (內文):
This project examines the narrative of death in New York City, a story currently suspended between two disparate islands: Manhattan and Hart Island. For over a century, Hart Island has served as the municipal burial site for the city's unclaimed, but today it faces an existential threat from severe coastal erosion, sea-level rise, and a rapidly depleting burial capacity. Conversely, Manhattan has legally prohibited human burials south of 86th Street since 1850, leaving the island as a sterile void in terms of the cycle of life and death. By identifying 161 Maiden Lane—a stalled construction project trapped in litigation with a compromised foundation—we saw an opportunity to bring the narrative of death back to this occupied space, transforming it into a vertical production hub that embraces the concept of designing with instability.  Section 2: 材質與現象實驗Heading (主標): The Accumulation of Presence and Absence
Subheading (副標): Material Studies & The Logic of Absorption
[Image: 02_concept_and_material_study]
Body (內文):
To ground our design in physical reality, we investigated the absorption phenomena between paper, ink, salt, and water, studying how matter is transported, transformed, and transferred. Through our operative drawings, we observed how varying salt concentrations, paper fiber densities, and ink-to-water ratios dictated the rate of absorption. This study revealed the shifting states of "absence" and "presence"—the invisible becoming visible—through the evaporation of water, the crystallization of salt, and the coagulation of ink. Ultimately, we found that salt is the critical catalyst in this cycle, governing the structural transitions and determining how fast materials are absorbed and transformed.  Section 3: 領土尺度與歷史變遷Heading (主標): The Growth & Decline of IslandsSubheading (副標): Mapping the Urban Paradox[Image: 03_manhattan_hart_island_map]Body (內文):A continuous metabolic exchange has dictated the growth and decline of these two islands. Historically, burials on Hart Island have been organized within deep, linear trenches systematically stacked with pine coffins. Over time, as organic matter decomposes and the earth settles, these subterranean grids subtly reveal themselves above ground, translating invisible architecture into an undulating, living topography. This territorial mapping illustrates the flow of materials, redefining boundaries and generating new coastlines across the urban archipelago.Section 4: 系統與代謝機制Heading (主標): The Logistical Machine
Subheading (副標): Bridging the Two Islands
[Image: 04_metabolic_mechanism_diagram]
Body (內文):
A synchronized mechanical system facilitates the exchange between these two islands. Hart Island acts as a resource generator, growing and exporting bamboo and wood chips, alongside crystallized salt generated from the reverse osmosis desalination process. Back at 161 Maiden Lane, the machine docks and utilizes a vertical transport system. It receives the imported plants, wood chips, and salt, distributing them efficiently into the vertical ecosystem and the Natural Organic Reduction (NOR) facility to fuel the continuous cycle.  Section 5: 曼哈頓節點設計 (剖面)Heading (主標): 161 Maiden Lane
Subheading (副標): Adaptive Reuse & Production Site
[Image: 05_161_maiden_lane_section]
Body (內文):
Translating this metabolic flow to an urban scale, we propose introducing a Natural Organic Reduction (NOR) facility within the paused exoskeleton of 161 Maiden Lane. This system will process approximately 1,500 bodies annually for New York City, generating a substantial volume of nutrient-rich soil. For the system's intake, we are partially dismantling the upper structure to create a vertical ecosystem. Shaped by solar carving logic to ensure the precise sunlight hours required for plant survival, this vertical garden provides the essential organic roots and carbon material needed for the NOR process while aiding in climate regulation. Rather than proposing a static architectural object, this intervention acts as a macro-scale urban infrastructure. Section 7: Hart Island 景觀與基礎設施Heading (主標): Restorative Infrastructure
Subheading (副標): Legacy and Preservation on Hart Island
[Image: 07_hart_island_section]
Body (內文):
Our ultimate goal is to sustain the legacy and history of burials on Hart Island. The nutrient-rich soil produced at 161 Maiden Lane is exported to expand its cemetery; by raising the ground elevation with this new soil, we provide critical resistance against coastal erosion. As the island becomes more public, our master plan integrates passenger ferries with vessels transporting remains and soil, with distinct pathways leading toward coastal infrastructure and burial trenches. Along the shoreline, buoyant desalination devices harvest clean water, while a 'telescopic' structural unit introduces much-needed shelters and restroom facilities that adapt to the shifting topography.  Section 8: 結論與實體模型Heading (主標): A Poetic Infrastructure
Subheading (副標): Redefining the 21st-Century Cemetery
[Image: 08_overall_physical_model]
Body (內文):
Our mobility system redefines the 21st-century cemetery: no longer a static plot, but a territorial infrastructure. By linking the production at 161 Maiden Lane to the restoration of Hart Island, we transform the urban cycle of death into a proactive tool for land reclamation. Through drawings and physical models that blend technical precision with poetic narrative, we envision a future where the city's metabolic flow protects and sustains its most vulnerable landscapes.
